# VXIII Brand Story

## 1. The name

VXIII is one word and one reference, Matthew 5:13. It is always written and read together, with no space and no mark between the letters. Behind the word, the V stands for the chapter and the XIII for the verse, so a single word carries the whole citation.

The verse opens with the line "Ye are the salt of the earth" (KJV). It goes on to say that salt which loses its savour has nothing left to give. We take that sentence as the premise of the brand: a person is worth what they keep of themselves.

The next verse, 5:14, says "Ye are the light of the world." VXIII sits one verse before the light. The wearer is the salt, and the frame is what they put on before they walk out into it.

## 2. The idea in one line

Keep your savour.

## 3. Why salt, and why sunglasses

Salt and sunglasses share more ground than it first seems.

**Salt is made by the sun.** Sea water is left in shallow pans under hard light until only crystal remains. The thing we build to face is the thing that makes salt. The sun is the maker, and the frame is how the wearer meets it.

**Salt preserves.** In the old world a covenant was sealed with salt because nothing else would last as long. A frame bought once and kept for years follows the same logic, and our promise to the buyer is written as a covenant.

**Salt works quietly.** Nobody sees salt in a meal, and people notice only when it is missing. VXIII is worn the same way, with the mark placed where only the wearer sees it, and a frame is judged by how little it announces.

**Salt has a measure.** The word salary comes from the salt that Roman soldiers were paid, and the phrase "worth your salt" still means someone has earned their place. We speak to people who intend to be worth it.

## 4. Who we speak to

We speak to people who buy few things and keep them. They value a good material, a clean line and a quiet detail, and they do not need an object to announce them.

They arrive through a friend, through the verse, or through the frame itself. Most already own sunglasses that they have lost or outgrown and want one pair that stays. We write for them as equals, with the plain confidence of someone who has nothing to prove.

## 6. The story

There is a verse that most people know by its first line and forget by its second. You are the salt of the earth. The second line is the one that matters. If the salt has lost its savour, what is left to season anything with?

We built VXIII on the second line.

Salt does not decorate. It is added to something so that the thing becomes more itself. A good cook uses very little and the whole dish changes. A frame can do the same for a face. It carries no slogan across the lens and it sits close to the skin. It is made of titanium, a metal that is slow to mark, and it is cut with the clean, hard edge of a crystal.

Salt is born in the sun, and that is the right place to begin a pair of sunglasses. The light is strongest at the hour when the pans are drying, and that is the hour this brand is made for.

We number the verse and not a product line, because the reference is the promise. VXIII is read as one word. Anyone who knows the verse will recognise it. Anyone who does not will ask, and the answer is a good one to be asked for.

You are the salt of the earth. Keep your savour. Put the frame on and go out into the light.

## 7. Manifesto

You are the salt of the earth.

You were not made to be loud. You were made to change the thing you are added to.

The sun made the first crystal and it will test you too. Meet it with something that holds its edge.

Keep your savour.

VXIII. Matthew 5:13.

## 9. Voice

- Plain declarative sentences, with one idea in each.
- Scripture appears at the name, in the manifesto and in the engraving. Everywhere else the voice is modern and direct.
- Salt words are used as ordinary vocabulary and are never forced into a pun.
- No exclamation marks, no emoji and no discount language.
- Short captions in capitals, as on the spec lines of a product page.

## 11. Colourways

Colourways take their names from salts and from the stages of making them. Each name describes a finish and a lens together. Colour is applied to the titanium by anodising or by PVD coating where the finish requires it.

| Name | Salt reference | Finish and lens |
|---|---|---|
| Fleur | Fleur de sel, the first and finest crystals off the pan | Polished silver titanium, clear grey lens |
| Gris | Sel gris, grey salt raked from the clay | Brushed gunmetal titanium, smoke lens |
| Noir | Black lava salt | Matte black titanium, deep grey lens |
| Rose | Rose rock salt | Rose gold titanium, soft amber lens |
| Ambre | Smoked salt | Warm gold titanium, amber lens |

The final selection for each model is set in the product range document.

## 12. Signature details

1. **The cube.** Halite crystallises as a cube, so the temple tip, the hinge block and the end piece are cut as small cubes with true edges.
2. **The engraving.** "VXIII", written as one word with no dots or spaces, and a short line from the verse, set on the inner face of the temple.
3. **The grain texture.** A fine matte finish on the temple, set against polished rims.
